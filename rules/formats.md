# Formats

A format is a whole-video treatment. Pick one per video, build it from the moves in moves.md, and hold it to the rules in the other files. Each format lists its STRUCTURE (what it is made of) and what it NEEDS before it can be made well.

## Talking head, clean

STRUCTURE: the speaker full frame, soft-build captions (CAP-04), an optional hook card from frame 0, zero to two graphics a minute, a spoken call to action if the speaker gave one, and punch-ins on payoff lines only. The screen mode is face: nothing takes the picture from the speaker. It fits raw opinion, rants, personal story, strong delivery, or a breather between graphic-heavy videos. Triage (CUT-27) picks the lane: full treatment, tight cut only, or hand-off to a human editor.

NEEDS: a take with a findable hook, and a 4K source if punch-ins are wanted (GFX-05).

## Talking head with graphics

STRUCTURE: the speaker full frame plus echo graphics (cards on a solid ground, slams, lists) that land on the words. Full-screen slides and phone screens keep the speaker in a picture-in-picture box. B-roll inserts, and at least two moves per video. The screen mode is light: overlays carry it, and what is said decides how many full-screen moments are earned (GFX-01, GFX-02). Something new lands at least every 2.5 s (GFX-04). Variants:
- Chapter list: numbered tabs up front, each item revealed a different way, with an example after each item. It suits `N things` videos.
- Tier list: a board builds as the speaker ranks, logo chips drop into tiers, and items move when the speaker changes their mind.
- Versus: a donut for shares, bars for numbers, compare boxes for head-to-heads.
- Stage and box: a graphics stage on top, the speaker in a rounded box with the whole head in frame, a header that types in per section, and a full-frame breather between runs. It suits walkthroughs of 3 to 6 sections.
- Split-screen explainer: a diagram in the top two-thirds drawing as the speaker talks, the speaker below, one-word captions on the seam, and full frame for punchlines.

NEEDS: a screen-plan step (GFX-01), a library of moves, and real assets for any proof (GFX-08, GFX-10).

## Podcast and multi-camera

No fixed template. The rules give these pieces to build from:
- A shot plan with one setup per line, rotating and filmed in 4K (CUT-24). A new shot about every 2.3 s is a pace to aim for.
- One grade base per camera, matched once, with both sides of every camera cut checked (COL-01, COL-02).
- Face lock per shot, with on-purpose walk-ins played as shot (CUT-25).
- A hook card held for the whole video at a compact size that steps aside for graphics (HOOK-13).
- The interview-answer clip: the best take of one answer, with the question as the text hook.
- Every line is the on-camera voice (CUT-18).

NEEDS: speaker identification across mics, angle-switch rules, a way to choose short moments from a long recording (CUT-19), and guest release handling. Define these with the user before the first video.

## Story and voiceover montage

STRUCTURE: a b-roll edit with no speaker. A hook line from frame 0, then 6 to 12 shots of 1.5 to 4 s, one idea per shot, with voiceover lines landing on the shots they name. Each shot has its own entrance and a slow push, captions come from the voiceover, and your own end sting closes the tail if you have one. Something new lands at least every 2.5 s (GFX-04), so a shot over 2.5 s carries a graphic or a cutaway inside it. Shots play silent, so the voiceover, the music bed and any clicks carry the sound (BR-13). Two variants:
- Cinematic story: dramatised scenes with a line of text over film. AI imagery is for objects and environments only, and people come only from real footage (BR-18).
- Text hook on b-roll: one line over one clip for 6 to 10 s, one lockup, music on. The pace gate only reports on videos under 8 s.

NEEDS: a clip index with descriptions, and the voiceover words with timings.

## Screen walkthrough

STRUCTURE: the recording in a rounded panel inside the platform safe zone with the browser chrome cropped, a kicker line above, captions below, and the speaker's face bubble kept. A cold-open hook is pulled from later in the take. Fillers, restarts and rambling are out, with joints of 0.10 to 0.15 s (CUT-10). Captions run word by word with one accent word. Punch-ins land on the on-screen element being described: zoom 1.2 to 1.75, held 0.8 s or more, with the element staying put as it grows. Run the clean audio chain (SND-02). Conform the audio to the container's timestamps first, because a recording can drift (0.2 s over 213 s was measured). Never auto-grade a screen recording: a dark-theme page reads as footage.

NEEDS: real screens, never mocked proof (GFX-10).

## B-roll montage, no voiceover

STRUCTURE: 3 to 6 clips from one subject or energy, cut on the beat of the music bed, slowed to about 85%, exposure pulled a third of a stop, no blur. Overlay text is in your brand's voice: offer three options and let the user choose one. Music is on. Footage comes from the user's own library, and AI stills never stand in for a place.

NEEDS: a library with subject tags and a keep and cut review (FOO-13).

## Quote card, reel and static

STRUCTURE: for a reel, the quote sits centred in the frame and the clip behind it is a dimmed, sharp (never blurred) backdrop for feeling, not a word-for-word illustration. Safety and brand checks still apply to the clip. The music plays through the end card and fades with it (SND-08). For a static, a 4:5 post in one of three rotating treatments of one brand system: a full-bleed hero photo with the copy on a scrim, a framed object such as a tilted card on your brand ground, or a typographic spotlight with centred type, a ghost brand glyph and one accent. Never two in a row in one treatment (DEL-16).

NEEDS: a source check for every quote or verse before posting.

## Further formats

- Metaphor system. STRUCTURE: one central symbol (a loop, a machine, a funnel, a map) carries the whole framework. Steps are numbered around it, themed props act as bullets and examples, and a recap lands on the symbol. NEEDS: its own symbol asset for each topic.
- Proof stack. STRUCTURE: it opens on data or a result, then stacks evidence: notifications, profile pills, big numbers, screenshot cards and a testimonial. NEEDS: real assets only (GFX-10).
- Brand spot. STRUCTURE: a 15 to 30 s voiceover over product UI, with no speaker. NEEDS: real product screens and a voiceover.
- Teardown. STRUCTURE: the ad or video under discussion is framed as a card with tracking boxes, callouts and a thought bubble for what the viewer thinks. NEEDS: the clip itself. Prefer your own or client work. Anyone else's clip stays short and is always commentary.
- Named-taxonomy video, about 30 s. STRUCTURE: seven beats. Announce the count. List the three, one per breath. Zoom into the one that matters on b-roll. Define it in the viewer's own logic. Turn back to the speaker on a `but the reality is` line. Contradict what the viewer already believes. Give the prescription. The three names are the only accent words. Cut to b-roll exactly on the zoom and back on the turn: two cuts carry the structure. One idea only. NEEDS: b-roll for the one name that matters.
