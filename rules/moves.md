# Moves

The vocabulary of single editing devices a format is built from, grouped by what they do. A move marked (real assets) is built only from the user's real footage, screens or stills, never mocked. Which moves a renderer can draw is that renderer's own list. Every move still obeys the rules in the other files.

## Cut and camera

- Hard cut: the default join between kept segments, with a gap of 0.0 to 0.10 s.
- Emphasis zoom: a 1.12 to 1.22 scale-up on a payoff line, with the face still visible.
- Sentence punch: a hard punch of about 1.4x at a sentence change, held through the sentence and gone on the next cut. It fakes a second camera from the 4K file.
- Sentence push: a slow push of about 12% across one whole sentence, never faster than 8% a second, held to the next cut.
- Pull-out: opens tight, about 1.2x, on a cut and eases back to the full frame across one sentence.
- Close push: a slow push of about 1.18x across the last sentence, held to the end.
- Walk-in open: the speaker walks into a medium shot while saying the first line. Filmed, never faked (real assets).
- Multi-angle cut: a hard cut to a tighter crop or a second spot of the same speaker.
- Face lock-on: the frame follows the head so the face stays pinned, with a strength from 0 to 1.

## Layout

- Picture-in-picture box: the speaker sits in a small box while a slide, phone or scene holds the frame.
- Split screen: a diagram fills the top two-thirds, the speaker sits below, and captions run on the seam.
- Board beside the speaker: the speaker stands to one side and a board pops up in the open space, sized so it never covers them.
- Stage and framed box: a graphics stage on top, the speaker in a rounded box below with the whole head in frame, and captions in the gap. It holds for a whole section.
- Screen with a face bubble: a screen recording with the speaker's face in a bubble.
- Text behind the speaker: big type sits behind the person's cut-out (needs a person matte).
- Stage breather cut: inside a stage-and-box video, the thesis line hard cuts to a full-bleed tight crop of the same take under plain captions, then cuts back on the next noun.

## Lists and reveals

- Blur reveal: upcoming items sit blurred and sharpen on the word that names them.
- Empty slots: blank numbered slots fill in as each item is said.
- Chapter tabs: numbered tabs persist across sections. The active one lights in your accent colour and the rest dim.
- Ghost-to-lit: a whole line sits faint and each word lights as it is said.
- Stacked tags: labels stack one under another as they are said.
- Typewriter build: a line types in as it is said, with the key blanks highlighted.
- This-or-that board: every pair sits on one blurred board. Each row sharpens as it is asked, the pick is circled and the other side dims.
- Two-column build: A and B columns built line by line as each is said.
- For and not-for list: check and cross lists of who it suits, with the key words underlined.
- Bracket formula: the ingredients of one idea build left to right in square brackets, each landing on its spoken word.
- Themed bullets: the bullets are props from the metaphor, such as chips, plates or coins.
- Check and cross chips: when an on and an off are said in one breath, a check chip and then a cross chip pop under the caption on their words.
- Progress rail: a thin track under the captions fills across the whole video, with a labelled dash added as each section starts.

## Captions and type

- One-word captions: one huge word at a time, centred or on the seam of a split.
- Punch words: single words slam on the beat and the key word takes your accent colour.
- Echo graphics: graphics repeat the speaker's words and act as the captions, so the caption line steps aside.
- Tiered captions: size and weight tiers per word, with hero and keyword levels.
- Statement line: one bold statement that builds line by line on the speaker's words.
- Negative-colour phrase: the bad-news phrase (two to four words) in a third caption colour, each word landing on its own word time.
- Payoff caption: the key phrase switches caption style for a beat, heavier and bigger.
- Title with a highlight bar: a headline with a bar behind the key phrase.
- Tracking animation: letters spread wide tighten into the word.

## Diagrams and data

- Boards: flow, tier, math, steps and dots boards.
- Live diagram: a curve or graph draws on the word and labels pop onto its points.
- Area fill: the curve fills in as the idea lands.
- Box comparison: two outlined boxes with a value, a label and a winner.
- Logo tier board: ranked rows where items drop in on the spoken word with their real logo or an icon, the row flashes, and items can move tiers.
- Donut fill: a donut fills slice by slice as each share is said, and legend rows land with it.
- Growing bars: bars grow on the spoken words, values land at the end, and the winner glows.
- Big number with a ghost label: a huge number with its label ghosted behind it.
- Loop diagram: a dashed loop ties a centre card to the nodes around it.
- Data race: a bar-chart race where one bar lights up.
- Days-later dial: a ruler slides to show time passing.
- Reach radar: a profile at the centre with rings and dots spreading out.
- Data overlay: a second line sweeps over the first while a counter ticks.
- Path metaphor: two paths, where a spotlight follows A and then the road snaps to B.

## Proof (real assets only)

- Notification stack: real payment or lead notifications pop and stack beside the speaker.
- Profile pill: a handle, follower count and post count pop near the person.
- Screenshot card: a real post or screenshot floats as a card with depth.
- View badge: a big view-count pill on an example clip.
- Proof split screen: two examples side by side, where the one being discussed lights up and the other dims.
- Rapid receipts: half-second flashes of proof with the number huge.
- Real product UI: bright real screenshots of members, calendars or dashboards.

## Transitions

- Band wipe: a diagonal band wipes to the next scene, its edge in your accent colour.
- Whip: a motion-blur whip into the next section.
- Mono drop: on the cut that starts the punchline, one wash frame, then hard black and white for the phrase, then back to colour on the next cut.
- Glass shatter: a small inset picture card breaks into glass shards on the bad-news word.
- Light streak: a light streak wipes across the frame.
- Push into an object: the camera dives into an icon or a screen.
- Shape-morph chain: text becomes a pill, then a notification, an icon and a logo.
- Velocity-matched seams: zoom-through, rack-focus blur cut and waterfall entry, where speed and direction carry across the cut.

## Scenes and inserts

- B-roll cutaway: real footage on a wipe, labelled, one clip per batch.
- Card stage: b-roll leaves full frame and plays in a rounded card on a softly lit ground, swapping sides beat to beat while captions carry on in the empty half.
- Analogy scene: the story as film shots with one line of text (real footage, AI for environments only).
- Environment swap: the speaker cut out into another world for a joke or a contrast (an AI-made environment).
- Meme or movie insert: a short pop-culture clip with the line typed over it (own or licensed footage, kept short).

## Annotation

- Hand annotation: a scribbled word with an arrow, percent markers or a timeline bar.
- Annotated clip: tracking boxes highlight words inside an example clip.
- Thought bubble: what the viewer is thinking.
- Callout lines: dashed lines tie a label to a part of the clip.
- Bracket labels: labels hang off an arc bracket around a graphic.

## Structure, call to action and UI

- Name tag: the speaker's name on an accent chip with the role beside it, once, right after the hook.
- Locked chapter header: the section's title on a bar at the top. It types in when a section starts and stays until the next.
- Giant step number: a big step number slammed over a close-up of the central symbol.
- Recap on the system: every step placed around the symbol or board at the end.
- Designed call to action: the call as its own scene, such as a numbered path with the comment keyword.
- Spoken call to action with a card: a graphic that backs the spoken call.
- Link pop-up: on `click the link below`, a pill rises on a long smooth ease-out, a finger taps, and an arrow glides down toward the link, with a tick and a click in word gaps.
- Phone screens: SMS, notifications, search, DM, profile, calendar and landing page.
- Edge-entry cards: proof or stat cards slide in from the frame edges and stack on the stage.
- Glow focus: a soft glow in your accent colour behind the number or element being discussed.
- Cursor click: a cursor moves in and clicks a real button.
- Skeleton to content: grey loading rows fill in with real content.
- Live counters: numbers tick up, only to a value the speaker says.
- Scarcity pill: a limited-spots pill flips state, with real numbers only.
- Focus pull per item: each row sharpens and lifts as it is named while the rest blur.
- Icon dock: a dock of icons, each opening into its real screen.
