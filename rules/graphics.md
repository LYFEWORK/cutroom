# Graphics and overlays

Read before planning, building or checking any overlay, card, graphic, camera move or full-screen scene.

## Screen plan

- **GFX-01** Overlays (captions, accents, zooms, cards, slams, name tag) keep the speaker on screen. A full-screen scene is earned by a line that points at something to show. Each video gets a mode: face (nothing takes the picture from the speaker), light (a few short moments, about one per 20 s) or full (as many as the format needs). (judgment)
- **GFX-02** Caps on full-screen moments. In full mode, allow at most the larger of 3 and one per 5 s of video, with 1.0 s or more of the speaker between two (0.75 s for footage-led videos). Scenes under 0.6 s apart count as one moment. A light moment runs no longer than its set length.

## Timing and camera

- **GFX-03** One idea per scene, with short text readable in under 2 s. Every element lands on the word it illustrates, about 0.05 s early. A card that arrives late or lingers fails. (judgment)
- **GFX-04** Something new is on screen at least every 2.5 s, the hook window included. New means a new picture (the user's real footage of what is said first, an AI clip only where nothing real fits) or a graphic that shows something. A camera move never counts, and a longer still stretch fails.
- **GFX-05** Camera moves live inside one shot. Three are allowed. A slow push (about 1.12, up to 1.18 on the last sentence, never faster than 8% a second) runs across one sentence and holds to the next cut. A pull-out (about 1.2) opens tight on a cut. A hard punch (about 1.4x) lands at a sentence change and holds through the sentence. Read them from the 4K file as a second camera: a 1080 take gets none. Allow one move a sentence at most, never on two sentences in a row, and never inside the hook window. A move never reverses in view, and never snaps in and out every few seconds or on every jump cut.
- **GFX-06** Punch-ins and emphasis zooms land on payoff lines only and are held through the line. An emphasis zoom is 1.12 to 1.22 for 0.9 s or more with the face visible, and it is suppressed while a card or slam is up. It is a camera move and obeys GFX-05. (judgment)

## What a graphic carries

- **GFX-07** Premium pop-ups, never little icons: real UI cards, devices and objects beside the speaker that act out the line (typing, tapping, filling, swiping). They land with a subtle click or tick in a gap between words. They arrive on one long smooth ease-out with a little blur and rise, never a bouncing spring. Small flat glyphs in round badges read cheap. (judgment)
- **GFX-08** A graphic is never a wireframe. Every card, panel or mockup carries real content: footage or stills from the library, real service names, details in plain words, never placeholder bars or empty fields. Examples rotate across trades so no section leans on one. (judgment)
- **GFX-09** Every graphic reads alone with the sound off. No filler labels (`or`, `versus`), every number says what it counts (`under 2 minutes`), no sentence chopped into fragments, and no shorthand that reads as something else. (judgment)
- **GFX-10** Every number on screen is one the speaker says or follows from their words (a total or a difference is the one exception). Stats slam in and never count up. A counter ticks only to a spoken value. Nothing invented ever appears: no names, reviews, results, client claims or fake proof screens.
- **GFX-11** Lists put the whole skeleton on screen when the list starts. Upcoming rows stay blurred (blank slots when a row is an answer or a pick) and sharpen on the word that names them. The board is revisited between items, and a top-N counts down to the best. No item sharpens more than 1.5 s before it is said unless it lands on its cue (`number two`).

## Placement

- **GFX-12** One top-zone element and one full-screen element at a time. A top-zone card never starts inside the hook window and ends at least 22 px above the crown. A card may shrink to clear the head, never below 0.72 of its size. If the room above the crown is under 0.85 of the height the card needs, split it or cut rows instead.
- **GFX-13** Cards, panels and overlays never cover a face or the top of the head in any frame. During a full-screen scene the speaker stays in a small picture-in-picture box (about x 60 to 360, y 1180 to 1560), with the graphic above y 1150. A split keeps the whole head below the seam. (judgment)
- **GFX-14** Full-screen scenes run about 2 to 6 s. A longer one keeps performing, with items landing on words, and never sits static. Stage sections last about 5 s or more, with a full-frame breather roughly every 25 s. (judgment)

## Motion and restraint

- **GFX-15** Motion lands premium: one long smooth ease-out with a little blur and rise. Entrances condense from soft to sharp with no spring overshoot, and exits take 0.3 s or less. Nothing draws in rigidly: a chart line flows along its length with the tip riding it, and a held slide keeps a slow push of about 3%. (judgment)
- **GFX-16** Premium means restraint. Use your own palette only, no stacked stat cards, odometer counters or drawn circles. End cards are your own and are never designed per video. (judgment)
- **GFX-17** The face stays on screen for at least 50% of the runtime of a talking-head video.

## Variety and craft

- **GFX-18** A reference means its look. When the user sends a video and says make this for a brand, build the same layout, mechanic and motion with that brand's colors, type, logo and product. Offer any reinterpretation as a one-line pitch beside it, never as the build. (judgment)
- **GFX-19** Videos in a batch never look the same twice. Each gets its own lead move and mix: pop-ups that shift through one thought at a time, AI clips of objects or places with the idea on a card, splits, slams. Use at most one list a video. The hook look and its spot stay constant (HOOK-11). (judgment)
- **GFX-20** Text renders ultra sharp at full output resolution, and every check frame the user sees is at full resolution too.
- **GFX-21** Boards pick themselves. Ranking gets a tier board with real logos, shares get a donut, numbers across options get bars, and a head-to-head gets a compare board. A speaker standing to one side gets the board in the open space beside them. (judgment)
- **GFX-22** Mono drop: the bad-news line drops to harsh black and white for its moment. Use it at most about once a minute, never twice within 8 s, never under a full-screen scene, and never on the fix or the win. (judgment)
