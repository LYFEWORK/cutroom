# Hook

Read before writing, building or checking a hook, and before placing anything in the first seconds of a video.

## Rules

- **HOOK-01** The hook card is fully formed from frame 0, with no entrance, and holds 3.0 s or more. It ends with the first sentence and holds 6.5 s at most.
- **HOOK-02** A working title is a label, never a hook. A hook makes a stranger stop: the subject and the tension land in one read, and the why is held back. Find it by reading the whole take for its tension. (judgment)
- **HOOK-03** The default is the triple hook: the on-screen text, the first spoken line and the picture in the first 3 s say the same thing. The text is the spoken line's own words, whole or with words dropped and order kept, never a new word. Stacked text, where the card speaks to the viewer instead of repeating the speaker, is an A/B option (HOOK-17), never the default. (judgment)
- **HOOK-04** Sound-off test: tile the first 6 s at 4 fps on one 6 x 4 sheet and ask whether a stranger could say what the video is about within 5 s. Sound-on test: every hook-text word is spoken in the first 5 s (FIX if not), with a CHECK when the order differs or the first spoken word starts after 1.5 s.
- **HOOK-05** Length: aim for 8 words or fewer and cap at 14. The card is 3 lines or fewer of 27 characters or fewer, with one accent phrase. A spoken opener is 14 words or fewer.
- **HOOK-06** The information gap: the viewer understands the subject and the tension but still needs the video for the why. Test every line for the one-second read, the so-what, the open loop and credibility (the video really supports it). Statements beat questions. (judgment)
- **HOOK-07** A callout hook speaks to someone: who they are, their pain, the moment they are in, and a promise of another way. A generic who with nothing at stake is a template. (judgment)
- **HOOK-10** A hook never uses a generic template (`3 tips for`), empty mystery, fake shock, jargon, `not X, it's Y`, a rhetorical question followed by its answer, an invented scene, hype words, or the full answer. (judgment)
- **HOOK-11** Keep one hook look, one spot and one size for every video of a brand. Never rotate looks or move the card per video. Looks on offer: white card, accent card, dark card, big open type, key words on a highlight bar. (judgment)
- **HOOK-12** A hook card never covers a face (anyone's, forehead to chin) in any frame of the hook window. It never touches the top of the head, hair included. Check the render on every frame, zooms and punch-ins counted. The card ends at least 22 px above the crown (aim for 24). If your hook spot would cover a face, go in order. First, raise the spot for every video, if it still clears the platform's top 220 px. Second, put that video's hook mid-frame (for example under the chin, about y 1320 on a 1080 x 1920 frame) and record the exception. Third, ship that video with no hook card.
- **HOOK-13** Nothing competes with the hook card in the hook window: no top-zone card, and no full-screen scene unless the format opens on a visual. A new picture of what is being said may still land there, because something new must arrive at least every 2.5 s (GFX-04). A hook held for the whole video uses a compact size, steps aside 0.15 s before each scene and returns 0.15 s after it. If two scenes are less than 1.2 s apart, the card stays away between them.
- **HOOK-14** Open the cut on the strongest straight line: the viewer's situation or the claim, stated flat. If the natural opener is weak, start on the first real sentence. Cold-open with a later line only if the video still flows and the line is not repeated. Never invent one. (judgment)
- **HOOK-16** The body stands on its own after any opener (no `like I said`), and its first line is a clean start. (judgment)
- **HOOK-17** Optional A/B: a hook version is the same body with its own spoken opener and card, so the triple and stacked text, or two openers, can be compared. Judge each by the 3 s hold: kill a version under 25% after a 72 h window. Skip rate is the hook's scorecard, and only a finding that repeats becomes a rule. (optional)
- **HOOK-18** Render a No Text copy, with no hook card, beside the hooked cut in the working files, so a hook can be typed in the platform's own app. (judgment)
- **HOOK-19** What is shown in the first 3 s literally shows the subject (the real screen, the thing, the result), never decoration and never a made-up scene. (judgment)
- **HOOK-20** Structural repost versions change exactly one variable. A cold open puts the most arresting line first. A cutdown is 25 to 40 s of the core beats with the same opener, ending on a finished sentence. Face only turns graphics off. Mirroring, 1.1x speed, brightness, grade, re-export and stickers are not versions. Never claim how a platform's duplicate detection works. (judgment)

## Writing (optional)

These help when you write hooks for the user. A video never fails delivery on them.

- **HOOK-08** A topic's five hooks use five levers, one each. Authority: a specific, real payoff. Relatability: that's me. Curiosity: a gap held open. Controversy: a belief the source really challenges. Story: a real test with the ending held back. (judgment) (optional)
- **HOOK-09** When the user supplies a hook formula or a creator's tip, write to that exact pattern in plain words, never a clever reframe of it. (judgment) (optional)
- **HOOK-15** Retention clock: stakes by 0:03, hook by 0:08, explain by 0:10, illustrate from 0:10 to 0:30, apply the rest. The close loops back to the opener, and every transition carries a named re-hook (an exception, a precondition, an announcement, a number tease, a contrast, a `but`). (judgment) (optional)
